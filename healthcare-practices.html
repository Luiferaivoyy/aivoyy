Find the revenue your practice may be leaving behind.

Healthcare practices can lose earned revenue when claims are incomplete, rejected, denied, underpaid, submitted late, or left without consistent follow-up. Patient balances, missing authorizations, incorrect information, unworked accounts, and inconsistent reporting can create additional financial leakage.

AI Voyy can help organize revenue-cycle information, identify patterns, prioritize follow-up, and give practice leaders clearer visibility into where attention may be needed.

Potential revenue-cycle opportunities

  • Claims or accounts with no recent activity
  • Denials and rejections awaiting follow-up
  • Filing and appeal deadlines approaching
  • Repeated denial reasons by payer or provider
  • Missing eligibility, referral, or authorization information
  • Potential underpayments
  • Unbilled or delayed encounters
  • Patient balances requiring appropriate follow-up
  • Write-off categories requiring management review
  • Revenue-cycle performance reporting

Revenue-cycle opportunities vary by practice type and may involve medical claims, dental claims, vision-plan billing, patient balances, client invoices, membership payments, treatment-plan payments, eligibility verification, or other accounts-receivable processes.

Illustrative revenue-cycle workflow

Claim or payment information received

Status and reason analyzed

Priority and deadline assigned

Follow-up, correction, or appeal prepared

Qualified staff reviews and takes action

Outcome tracked and reported

Qualified practice personnel must retain responsibility for reviewing and approving claim submissions, coding changes, appeals, clinical documentation, and other consequential actions.

Healthcare workflows require careful controls. AI Voyy evaluates each solution around the practice’s systems, data, access requirements, privacy obligations, and intended use. Solutions can include limited data access, role-based permissions, activity logging, human approval steps, and HIPAA-capable technology providers when protected health information is involved.

Human review

Billing, coding, clinical, financial, and other consequential decisions remain subject to review by qualified practice personnel.

Privacy by design

Each workflow is scoped to limit unnecessary access to patient, client, and practice information.

Controlled implementation

Solutions are tested, documented, and introduced through defined approval, monitoring, and escalation processes.

Appropriate technology

Technology providers and integrations are evaluated according to the information involved, the intended use, contractual requirements, and the practice’s security needs.
